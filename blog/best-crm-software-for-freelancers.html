Best CRM Software for Freelancers in 2026: 5 Tools Compared

By LedgerLane · November 23, 2026

Daniel is a freelance web designer in Denver. Last spring he had eleven active conversations — three proposals out, two discovery calls booked, a past client asking for a follow-up, and a referral he kept meaning to answer. He tracked all of it in his head, a spreadsheet, and a pile of unread emails.

Two of those proposals went cold. Not because the clients said no — because Daniel forgot to follow up, and by the time he remembered, they had hired someone else. A CRM exists to stop exactly that: one place where every client, lead, conversation and deal lives, that reminds you who to chase and never forgets. For a freelancer, the right one is small, cheap, and boring in the best way. Here are the five that actually fit a one-person business in 2026, with their real prices and their real limits.

The short answer: HubSpot CRM is the best free start for most freelancers — $0 for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with a real pipeline. Zoho CRM gives you the most features per dollar once you pay ($14 a user a month). Freshsales has the cheapest paid seat at $9 a month with phone and email built in. Pipedrive has the clearest pipeline but no free plan, and Capsule is the simplest of the five.

That is why the free-plan column matters more than the headline price: a $9-a-month plan that starts after a three-week trial costs you $108 in year one, while a $0 plan costs you nothing until you outgrow it.

1. HubSpot CRM — the best free start

HubSpot's free CRM is the most complete $0 tool on this list, and it is the default answer for a reason. It is free forever — not a trial — for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with no credit card required. You get a contact and company database, a deal pipeline board, email tracking (you see when a prospect opens your email), a meeting booking link, live chat, forms, and basic email marketing up to 2,000 sends a month. The free tier even includes Breeze, HubSpot's AI assistant, for researching companies and summarising records.

What makes it the best starting point is the upgrade path. When you outgrow free, you do not have to move your data anywhere — Starter is the same product with the limits lifted. Starter lists at $20 per seat a month, with a new-customer offer of $7 a seat a month on annual billing ($10 month to month). That is the cheapest first paid step of any CRM here if you catch the offer — but budget for the list price, because the offer is for new customers only and the price after it is $20.

The catch: the free limits are real and they arrive sooner than you expect. Two users means you cannot add a third person — a VA, a partner — without paying. One thousand contacts sounds like a lot until you import an old email list. HubSpot branding sits on your emails, forms and meeting links until you pay, you get only 10 custom fields, and anything serious — email sequences, sales forecasting, custom reports — needs Professional at $90 a seat a month on annual billing, plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee. That jump, from $0 or $20 to $90 plus $1,500, is the steepest ladder on this list.

Pick HubSpot if: you want a genuinely free CRM today and you are happy to stay inside its limits for a year or two.

2. Zoho CRM — the most CRM per dollar

Zoho is the value pick, and it has been for years. The free plan covers up to 3 users — one more than HubSpot — with leads, contacts, deals, tasks, workflow rules and a mobile app, capped at 5,000 records. When you pay, Standard is $14 per user a month on annual billing ($20 month to month), and it already includes email integration, mass email, built-in calling, up to five pipelines, sales forecasting and custom modules. Professional at $23 ($35 monthly) adds process management, quotes and customer portals; Enterprise at $40 ($50 monthly) adds the Zia AI assistant and territory management.

For a freelancer, the important comparison is with HubSpot's paid tiers. Zoho Standard at $14 gives you forecasting and multiple pipelines — features HubSpot reserves for its $90 Professional tier. And Zoho never charges an onboarding fee at any tier, which alone saves you $1,500 against HubSpot Professional.

The catch: Zoho is the least polished of the five — setup takes longer, the menus run deep, and it is the CRM most likely to make you feel you are administering software instead of selling. If you hate fiddly settings, the $14 price will not feel cheap for long.

Pick Zoho if: you want the deepest feature set for the lowest monthly price and you do not mind spending an afternoon setting it up properly.

3. Pipedrive — the clearest pipeline

Pipedrive is built around one idea: a deal moves left to right across a visual pipeline, and your job is to keep it moving. For a freelancer who sells a service in clear stages — enquiry, discovery call, proposal, negotiation, won — it is the most satisfying tool here. Lite costs $14 per seat a month on annual billing ($24 month to month) and covers lead, deal and contact management, calendar events and basic reporting. Growth at $39 adds full email sync, workflow automation and more reporting; Premium at $59 and Ultimate at $79 sit above that.

There is no free plan, only a 14-day trial, so the meter starts at $168 a year from day one. That is the same starting price as Zoho Standard, but with fewer features for the money — you are paying for the pipeline experience, not the feature list.

The catch: the email limit is the one that bites. Lite has basic email tools only — full two-way email sync, with tracking, starts on Growth at $39 a seat a month. For a freelancer whose whole sales process lives in email, that means the plan you actually need costs nearly three times the headline price. Pipedrive's plan names also changed in 2026 (Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate replaced the older Essential, Advanced, Professional and Enterprise names), so older reviews quote prices for plan names that no longer exist.

Pick Pipedrive if: you live in your pipeline, you sell in clear stages, and you are willing to pay $39 a month for the plan that actually syncs your email.

4. Freshsales — the cheapest paid seat

Freshsales, from Freshworks, is the cheapest way into a paid CRM with a phone built in. Growth is $9 per user a month on annual billing ($11 month to month) and includes a Kanban pipeline, contact lifecycle stages, chat, email and phone, email templates, custom fields and basic workflows. Pro at $39 ($47 monthly) adds contact scoring, deal insights and sales sequences; Enterprise at $59 ($71 monthly) adds forecasting and a sandbox.

Nine dollars a month for a CRM with a built-in phone is genuinely cheap — it is less than a streaming subscription, and it replaces a separate calling tool. For a freelancer who does outbound work, cold outreach or a lot of discovery calls, having the phone inside the CRM means every call is logged against the contact automatically, which is the kind of thing that quietly saves an hour a week.

The catch: there is no free plan. Freshworks removed it in November 2026, so any roundup still calling Freshsales free is wrong. You get a 21-day trial and then you pay. And the plan ladder is steep in a different way: sales sequences — the automated follow-up emails that stop leads going cold, which is the whole point for many freelancers — start on Pro at $39, more than four times the Growth price. If you buy Freshsales for the follow-up automation, budget $39, not $9.

Pick Freshsales if: you make a lot of calls, you want phone, email and chat on one $9 bill, and you do not need automated sequences yet.

5. Capsule — the simplest of the five

Capsule is the CRM for the freelancer who looks at HubSpot and Zoho and thinks: too much. It is built around contacts — every call, email, task and deal sits on the contact record — with one sales pipeline, tasks, and a clean, fast interface. The free plan covers 2 users and 250 contacts with one pipeline. Starter is $18 per user a month on annual billing ($22 month to month) and raises the contact limit to 30,000. Growth at $36 ($40 monthly) adds workflow automation, multiple pipelines and email sync; Advanced at $54 and Ultimate at $72 sit above that.

Capsule's real strength is that there is almost nothing to learn. You can set it up in an afternoon and actually use it the same day, which is more than can be said for Zoho. It also integrates neatly with accounting tools — handy if you already run your books in Xero or QuickBooks.

The catch: 250 contacts on the free plan is the tightest cap on this list — a freelancer with a few years of networking will outgrow it fast. And the paid ladder climbs quickly: workflow automation, which Zoho includes at $14 and Freshsales at $9, does not arrive until Capsule Growth at $36. You pay a premium for simplicity, and the premium grows as you need more.

Pick Capsule if: you want the smallest, calmest CRM that exists, and you are happy to pay $18 a month for that calm.

Which one should you pick?

It depends on how you sell, not on which tool is "best" in the abstract:

  • Starting from zero and want to pay nothing: HubSpot Free. Two users, 1,000 contacts, a real pipeline, no card. Move to Zoho Standard at $14 when you need forecasting or a third user.
  • Most features for the least money: Zoho CRM — free for 3 users, then $14 a month, the best value here if you can live with the interface.
  • You sell in clear stages and love a pipeline board: Pipedrive Lite at $14 — but go in knowing the plan that syncs your email properly is Growth at $39.
  • You live on the phone and want the cheapest paid seat: Freshsales Growth at $9, with phone, email and chat on one bill.
  • You want the simplest thing that works: Capsule. Free for 250 contacts, then $18 a month, and you will actually use it because it never gets in your way.

A CRM only works if the deals go in it. The freelancers who get value from these tools log every conversation the day it happens — the same discipline as sending the invoice the day the work lands, which our guide to how to invoice as a freelancer covers step by step. And once the money starts coming in, it needs somewhere clean to land — our guide to the best bank account for freelancers compares six free and low-cost business accounts, and QuickBooks vs Xero covers the bookkeeping side once your client list grows.

The bottom line

For most freelancers in 2026, the answer is HubSpot Free — $0, 2 users, 1,000 contacts, and a pipeline that works from day one. If you outgrow it, Zoho Standard at $14 a user a month is the best-value paid step, and Freshsales Growth at $9 is the cheapest paid seat if you make a lot of calls. Pipedrive is the best pipeline and Capsule the simplest tool, but both cost more than their headline prices suggest once you need email sync or automation. The expensive mistake is not picking the wrong CRM — it is picking none at all and letting another proposal go cold.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free CRM for freelancers?

HubSpot CRM. Its free plan covers up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts with a real deal pipeline, email tracking and a meeting booking link, with no time limit and no credit card. Zoho CRM is the other genuine free option, covering 3 users and 5,000 records. Pipedrive and Freshsales have no free plan — only 14-day and 21-day trials.

Is HubSpot CRM really free?

Yes, for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with no time limit. The limits are what to watch: a third user, more than 1,000 contacts, removing HubSpot branding, or email sequences all need a paid plan. Starter lists at $20 a seat a month, with a $7 new-customer offer on annual billing.

What is the cheapest CRM for a freelancer?

Freshsales Growth at $9 per user a month on annual billing is the cheapest paid plan here, and it includes phone, email and chat. But "cheapest" depends on what you need: HubSpot and Zoho are $0 to start, and if you need automated follow-up sequences, Freshsales Pro at $39 costs more than Zoho Standard at $14.

Do I need a CRM if I only have a few clients?

Yes — a few clients is exactly when a CRM pays off, because one forgotten follow-up can cost you a whole project. A free plan from HubSpot or Zoho costs nothing and takes an afternoon to set up. Spreadsheets work until about ten active conversations; after that, deals start slipping, usually the ones you most wanted to win.
