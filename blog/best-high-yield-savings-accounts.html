What is a high-yield savings account?

A high-yield savings account is a regular savings account that pays a much higher interest rate than the national average. Most HYSAs are offered by online banks. Because online banks do not pay for branch networks, they pass part of the saving to customers as higher rates and lower fees.

Three facts matter more than anything else:

  1. APY (Annual Percentage Yield) is the rate that actually counts. It includes compound interest, so it tells you what your money earns over a year if you leave it untouched.
  2. Your money is insured. At an FDIC-insured bank, deposits are protected up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank (credit unions are insured the same way by the NCUA). A HYSA at an insured online bank is exactly as safe as a savings account at a branch bank.
  3. Rates are variable. The APY can rise or fall when the Federal Reserve moves its benchmark rate. The account that tops the list today may not top it in three months — which is why this page is reviewed and updated regularly.

How much more does a HYSA actually earn?

On a $10,000 balance held for one year:

  • At 0.10% APY (typical big-bank rate): about $10 of interest
  • At 3.50% APY: about $350
  • At 4.40% APY: about $440

That difference — hundreds of dollars a year — requires no extra risk and no investing. It is simply the reward for parking the same money in a better account.

The accounts in detail

Axos ONE® Savings — up to 5.00% APY

The highest headline rate on this list. The 5.00% promotional rate comes with conditions: for example, maintaining an average balance of $1,500 with at least $1,500 in monthly direct deposits into a linked Axos checking account, or a $5,000 average balance with $5,000 in monthly transfers. The promotion runs for six months; after that, the standard rate (4.21% APY when requirements are met, per the latest published figures) applies. Best if you are willing to move your direct deposit.

Pibank Savings — 4.40% APY

One of the strongest straightforward offers: a high rate with no monthly fee. A good pick if you want a top-tier APY without juggling balance tiers or linked-account requirements. The trade-off is access: money goes in through Plaid or a wire, and withdrawals are by wire only — no ACH transfers, Zelle or bill pay. Pibank charges no wire fees, but your other bank may charge to receive one, so this account suits money you plan to park rather than move often.

SoFi Checking and Savings — up to 4.40% APY

SoFi combines checking and savings in one app, and the top savings rate is available to members who meet its direct-deposit or qualifying activity requirements. Best for people who want their everyday spending and savings in a single place and can route a paycheck through it. One detail the headline hides: the 4.40% is the 3.30% base APY plus a 1.10% boost that runs for up to six months on new accounts with eligible direct deposits (or $5,000 in qualifying deposits every 31 days). After the boost period the ongoing rate is 3.30% with direct deposit — and only 0.80% without it.

Openbank High Yield Savings — up to 4.35% APY

Openbank (Santander's digital brand) offers a clean, standalone savings account with one of the highest widely available rates. Best if you want to keep your existing checking account and simply add a high-yield home for savings.

CIT Bank Platinum Savings — up to 4.35% APY

CIT's top rate is aimed at savers who keep a larger balance in the account — the best tier is designed for balances of $5,000 and above. If your emergency fund is already built up, this is one of the strongest places to park it.

Peak Bank Envision High-Yield Savings — 4.27% APY

A $100 minimum deposit to open, and a strong rate if you are starting smaller than most savers. It is not the highest low-minimum rate on this list — Pibank pays 4.40% with no minimum at all — but Peak pairs its 4.27% with a more conventional banking experience.

An introductory 4.25% APY for the first six months. Most attractive if you already invest with E*TRADE and want savings and investments under one login.

How to choose the right HYSA for you

Work through these five questions in order:

  1. Can I meet the conditions? The very highest rates often require direct deposits or minimum balances. A slightly lower rate with no conditions beats a top rate you fail to qualify for.
  2. Is there a monthly fee? Most accounts above charge none. Never pay a monthly fee on a savings account in 2026.
  3. How fast can I get my money back? Transfers to an external bank typically take 1–3 business days. Keep true emergency cash accessible.
  4. Is the bank FDIC-insured? Confirm the insurance logo and the bank's legal name on its website before depositing.
  5. Does the app fit my habits? You will check this account for years. A clean app and easy transfers matter more than a 0.05% rate difference.

How to open a high-yield savings account (step by step)

  1. Choose the account using the comparison above and confirm today's APY on the bank's site.
  2. Apply online — you will need your Social Security number, a government ID, and your existing bank's routing and account numbers.
  3. Verify your identity (most online banks approve within minutes; some use small test deposits).
  4. Fund the account with an initial transfer.
  5. Set up an automatic monthly transfer from your paycheck — even $50 a month builds the habit and the balance.
  6. Re-check the rate every few months. If your bank's APY falls far behind the market, moving is free and takes an afternoon.

A quick word on taxes

Interest earned in a savings account is taxed as ordinary income in the US. Your bank will send you a Form 1099-INT if you earn more than $10 in interest in a year. This is not a reason to avoid a HYSA — earning $440 and paying tax on it still leaves you far ahead of earning $10 — but it is worth knowing before tax season.

Frequently asked questions

Are high-yield savings accounts safe? Yes — as long as the bank is FDIC-insured (or the credit union is NCUA-insured), your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per depositor, per institution, exactly like a traditional bank account.

Why do online banks pay so much more? They have no branch network to fund, and they compete nationally for deposits. Lower costs let them share more of the interest with customers.

Can the APY change after I open the account? Yes. HYSA rates are variable and usually move with the Federal Reserve's benchmark rate. Your bank must notify you of changes, and you can move your money at any time without penalty.

How much money should I keep in a HYSA? A common guideline is an emergency fund of 3–6 months of essential expenses. Money you need within a year — a tax bill, a car purchase, a deposit — also belongs in a HYSA rather than in investments that can fall in value.

Is there a catch with the 5.00% Axos rate? It is a promotional rate with balance and direct-deposit conditions, and it lasts six months. Read the conditions carefully; if you cannot meet them, a no-conditions account at 4.27–4.40% is the better deal.

The bottom line

In November 2026, leaving serious savings in a near-zero-rate account is an expensive habit. The strongest accounts on this list pay 4.20–5.00% APY, while the big-name options — Marcus (3.50%) and Capital One (3.20%) — pay less in exchange for brand trust and in-person access. Every account here charges no monthly fee and carries the same federal insurance as any bank. Pick the account whose conditions you can actually meet, automate a monthly transfer, and review the rate a few times a year.
