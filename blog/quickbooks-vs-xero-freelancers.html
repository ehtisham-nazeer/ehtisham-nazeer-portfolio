QuickBooks vs Xero for Freelancers (2026): Which One Actually Saves You Money?

By LedgerLane · November 21, 2026

Here is the uncomfortable truth about this comparison: the cheaper tool on the sticker is usually not the cheaper tool in year one. Xero's entry plan costs $11 a month less than QuickBooks' entry plan. But Xero's entry plan caps you at 20 invoices a month, and the moment you outgrow that cap you are paying $59 — $21 a month more than QuickBooks' equivalent. The right choice depends on how you actually bill, not on which logo you like.

Daniel, a freelance web developer in Denver, learned this the expensive way. He picked Xero Early at $27 a month because it was the cheapest real plan on either side. Four months in, his invoices crossed 20 a month, and Xero bumped him to Growing at $59. He had chosen the cheap plan and ended up paying more than QuickBooks Simple Start would have cost him from day one.

The short answer: For most US freelancers, QuickBooks Simple Start at $38 a month wins once you send more than 20 invoices a month, and it wins at tax time if you file Schedule C. Xero Early at $27 a month wins if you send 20 or fewer invoices and want unlimited users on your account. Ignore the brand debate — count your invoices first, then pick.

Two things jump out. First, Xero is cheaper at every comparable tier at list price — $27 vs $38, $59 vs $85, $97 vs $140. Second, QuickBooks has a cheaper floor: a genuinely free plan, and a $20-a-month Solopreneur plan built for self-employed Schedule C filers, with income tracking, mileage and quarterly estimated-tax estimates. Xero has no equivalent of either. If your accounting needs are truly minimal, QuickBooks can cost you $0 or $20. If your needs are real but small, Xero Early at $27 is the cheapest full accounting plan either company sells.

Round 1: Invoicing — where Xero's cap bites

This is the round that decides the purchase for most freelancers, and it is the one most comparisons bury.

Xero Early lets you send 20 invoices and enter 5 bills a month. That sounds generous until you count: a freelancer with eight clients on monthly retainers plus a few one-off projects crosses 20 invoices easily — Daniel did it in four months. Past the cap, your only move inside Xero is Growing at $59, more than double the price. Growing removes the limits entirely: unlimited invoices, quotes and bills.

QuickBooks Simple Start has no invoice cap at all. Unlimited invoices, estimates and receipt capture for $38 a month, plus 1099 preparation and mileage tracking.

The honest limitation, Xero's side: the Early plan is a starter plan with a hard ceiling, and the jump to Growing is steep — $27 to $59. If you are anywhere near 15 invoices a month when you sign up, start on Growing or pick QuickBooks, because you will be upgrading within a quarter.

The honest limitation, QuickBooks' side: the free plan is nearly unusable for a working freelancer, and Solopreneur at $20 is income tracking, not full double-entry accounting — it has no accountant access and only one user.

Round 2: Users and collaboration — Xero's big win

Xero charges no per-user licence fees. Every plan, including Early at $27, lets you invite unlimited users — your bookkeeper, your accountant, a virtual assistant, a business partner — at no extra cost.

QuickBooks charges by seats. Simple Start includes 1 user, Essentials 3, Plus 5 and Advanced 25. Your accountant is the exception — Simple Start, Essentials and Plus each include access for 2 accountants, and Advanced allows 3 — so a solo freelancer with one bookkeeper is fine on Simple Start. The cap bites when you add staff: a VA who needs to send invoices, a partner who needs to check the books. Each extra person can force a tier jump.

Round 3: Tax time — QuickBooks edges it for US filers

For a US freelancer, tax season is where accounting software either earns its fee or wastes it.

QuickBooks is built for the American tax return. Solopreneur ($20 a month) maps your income and expenses directly to Schedule C lines and feeds into TurboTax, which Intuit also owns. Simple Start adds 1099 preparation, sales-tax tracking and mileage. If your accountant already uses QuickBooks — and most US accountants do, through Intuit's ProAdvisor network — handing over your books at year-end is a non-event.

Xero handles the same jobs competently: W-9 and 1099 management, sales-tax calculation and bank reconciliation are on every plan, and its document capture (formerly Hubdoc) pulls receipts in automatically. What it does not have is QuickBooks' home-field advantage with US accountants and TurboTax.

Round 4: Getting paid, foreign clients and extras

Both tools let clients pay invoices online, and both charge payment-processing fees on card payments — check the live rate on the provider's page before you promise a client a number, because those rates move. Both connect to your bank and reconcile transactions automatically.

The differences are in the edges. Xero's Established plan ($97) includes multi-currency support, project time-and-cost tracking and employee expense claims. QuickBooks only gives you project profitability tools on Plus ($140). So a freelancer billing clients in euros or pounds pays $97 on Xero against $140 on QuickBooks — Xero wins the international round by $43 a month.

Inventory flips it: QuickBooks Plus includes inventory tracking; Xero's core plans do not. If you sell physical products alongside services, QuickBooks Plus is the natural home. Payroll is an add-on on both sides — Xero Payroll, powered by Gusto, is listed at $36 a month plus $6 per person; QuickBooks payroll starts at about $50 a month plus about $7 per employee. Add-on pricing changes often, so confirm before budgeting.

Frequently asked questions

Is Xero cheaper than QuickBooks? At list price, yes — at every comparable tier: Early ($27) beats Simple Start ($38), Growing ($59) beats Essentials ($85), and Established ($97) beats Plus ($140). But the plan you actually land on is what matters: a freelancer sending more than 20 invoices a month pays $59 on Xero against $38 on QuickBooks, so QuickBooks is cheaper for that freelancer.

Does Xero have a free plan? No. Xero offers a 30-day free trial and then charges from $27 a month. QuickBooks has a free plan at $0, but it allows only 2 invoices a month and no accountant access, so it suits a side project rather than a working freelance business.

Which is better for taxes? QuickBooks, for most US freelancers — its Solopreneur plan is built around Schedule C, it feeds into TurboTax, and most US accountants already work in it. Xero handles W-9s, 1099s and sales tax well, so if your accountant prefers Xero, there is no tax reason to switch.

Can I switch from one to the other later? Yes. Both let you export your data, and both have import tools for the other side. Expect a weekend of cleanup — categories never map perfectly — and reconcile at least one full month by hand after the move. Keep your old data exported before you cancel anything.

Do I need the top plan as a freelancer? Almost certainly not. Established and Plus exist for multi-currency billing, inventory and project tracking. If none of those apply to you, the entry or mid plan is the right size.

What if I only send a few invoices a month? Then the cheapest honest options are QuickBooks' free plan ($0, up to 2 invoices) or Xero Early ($27, up to 20). If you send 3–20 invoices and want full accounting, Xero Early is the cheapest real plan on either side — just watch the cap the way Daniel did not.

The bottom line

Count your invoices before you count your dollars. Twenty or fewer a month, US clients, and a bookkeeper who needs access: Xero Early at $27 is the best value in this comparison. More than twenty invoices, or a Schedule C return to file: QuickBooks Simple Start at $38 — or Solopreneur at $20 if you only need income and mileage tracking. International clients: Xero Established at $97 beats QuickBooks Plus at $140. And whichever you pick, pair it with a proper business bank account — see our freelancer bank account comparison — because clean books and clean banking are what let you build business credit later, instead of starting from zero when you need it.

Try both free trials with real data before you pay for either. Connect your bank, send a real invoice, run a profit-and-loss report. The right tool becomes obvious within a few days — and the wrong one announces itself the month your invoice count crosses a cap you forgot existed.
