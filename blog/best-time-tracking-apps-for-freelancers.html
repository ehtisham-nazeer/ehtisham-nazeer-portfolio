Best Time Tracking Apps for Freelancers in 2026: 5 Tools Compared

By LedgerLane · November 26, 2026

Daniel is a freelance web developer in Denver. He bills $85 an hour, works roughly 30 billable hours a week — and at the end of his first quarter freelancing, he could only account for about 22 of them. The other eight hours a week were real work: Slack messages, "quick" calls, revisions that ran long. They just never made it onto an invoice. At his rate, that was about $2,700 a month he was giving away for free.

That is what a time tracker is actually for. Not surveillance, not productivity theatre — a record of the hours you already worked, so you can bill them, price the next project honestly, and stop guessing which clients are worth keeping.

The quick version: For most solo freelancers, Clockify's free plan is the best place to start — it costs $0 for up to 5 users with unlimited tracking. If you need invoicing built in, Harvest's free plan covers one seat and two projects, or Clockify Standard at $5.49 a month. Toggl Track is the simplest paid upgrade at $9 a month; Everhour only makes sense inside Asana or Trello; Hubstaff is for teams whose clients demand proof of hours.

1. Clockify — the best free plan, and the cheapest paid one too

Clockify is the default recommendation for a freelancer who wants to start today without paying anything. The free plan covers up to 5 users with unlimited time tracking, timers, timesheets, an auto tracker, calendar integration and basic reports. That is a genuinely usable free tier, not a demo with the useful parts removed.

Paid plans start at Basic, $3.99 per seat a month on annual billing ($4.99 monthly), which adds billable rates, CSV and Excel exports, and a kiosk mode. But invoicing — turning tracked hours into an actual invoice — sits one rung higher, on Standard at $5.49 a month annual ($6.99 monthly), which also adds timesheet approvals, time off and a manager role. Pro, at $7.99 annual ($9.99 monthly), adds expenses, budgets, scheduling and forecasting. Enterprise is $11.99 annual ($14.99 monthly).

The limitation to know: the free plan's reports only cover 31 days at a time and export to PDF only, and billable rates are a paid feature — so if you want to see what a client's hours are actually worth, you need Basic at least. Clockify also gives no refunds on paid plans, so run the 7-day trial before paying for a year.

Best for: solo freelancers and tiny teams who want a free tracker that stays free, and the lowest-priced route to invoicing.

2. Toggl Track — the simplest tracker to actually keep using

Toggl Track wins on the thing no feature list captures: you will still be using it in six months. The timer is one click, it lives in the browser, on desktop and on mobile, and it syncs with Google and Outlook calendars. For a freelancer, that is the whole job — the tracker that gets used beats the tracker with the longer feature list.

The free plan covers up to 3 users with real-time and manual timers, focus mode, calendar integrations, tasks and boards, and personal reports. Starter costs $9 per user a month billed annually ($12 monthly) and adds billable rates, team reports, timeline views, tags and milestones. Premium is $16 a month annual ($24 monthly) and adds labour costs, profitability reports, fixed-fee projects, timesheet approvals and single sign-on. Enterprise is quoted. Both paid plans carry a 30-day trial.

One warning about the numbers: Toggl relaunched its plans in September 2026, and older reviews still show the previous prices — Premium at $18 and a free plan for 5 users. The figures above are from Toggl's current pricing page, read in November 2026. Time-off and capacity management are also sold as separate add-ons ($2 and $5 a month per user on annual billing), so check the total before you commit.

The limitation to know: Toggl Track has no invoicing at all. Tracked hours become reports, not bills — you still need an invoicing tool, which is why our guide on how to invoice as a freelancer pairs naturally with it.

Best for: freelancers who want the fastest, least-friction timer and are happy to invoice elsewhere.

3. Harvest — the one where tracking and invoicing are the same workflow

Harvest is built around a single loop: track the hours, turn them into an invoice, get paid. The free plan is $0 forever for one seat and two projects, and it already includes invoicing and expense tracking — the only free plan on this list where invoicing is not paywalled.

Teams costs $9 per seat a month billed annually ($11 monthly) with unlimited seats, team reporting and accounting integrations (Stripe, QuickBooks Online, Xero, Deel). Enterprise is $14 annual ($17.50 monthly) and adds profitability reporting, timesheet approvals, an activity log and single sign-on. Enterprise Plus is custom. Paid plans carry a 30-day trial with no card required.

Two things changed recently, and both matter. Harvest was acquired by Bending Spoons in July 2025, and on November 1, 2026 it replaced its old single Pro plan with the Teams/Enterprise split above. More importantly, paid plans now carry usage-based charges on top of the seat price — extra invoices, projects, clients and tasks are billed separately, and Harvest does not publish those rates on its pricing page. Some long-time customers reported very large renewal increases in 2026. The $9 headline is a starting rate, not a guaranteed total.

The limitation to know: the free plan's two-project cap is tight — a freelancer with four active clients hits it in the first week. And the unpublished usage fees mean a growing team should model the real bill before renewing, not after.

Best for: freelancers who bill by the hour and want the shortest path from timer to invoice, with one or two clients at a time.

4. Everhour — the tracker that lives inside your project board

Everhour is a different kind of tool: its timer sits inside Asana, Trello, Jira, ClickUp and Basecamp, next to the task itself. If your clients already run their work in one of those boards, you never open a second app to start tracking — and that is the entire pitch.

The free plan covers up to 5 seats with time tracking, projects, tasks, reports and export — but no integrations, which removes the reason most people choose Everhour. There is exactly one paid plan: Team, at $8.50 per seat a month billed annually ($10 monthly), with every feature included — native integrations, billable rates, budgets, invoicing, expenses, time approval, time off, scheduling and single sign-on. Custom pricing starts at 50 seats. There is a 14-day trial.

The catch is the seat rule: Team has a 5-seat minimum. A solo freelancer pays for five seats — $42.50 a month on annual billing, $50 monthly — even if only one person ever tracks time. That makes Everhour the most expensive option on this list for a solo freelancer, despite the second-lowest per-seat price. Annual plans also do not refund unused months if you cancel.

The limitation to know: outside Asana, Trello, Jira or a similar board, Everhour is an ordinary tracker at an extraordinary price for one person. It only earns its cost when the integration is the point.

Best for: small teams of five or more who already live in Asana or Trello and want budgets and invoices inside the board.

5. Hubstaff — the one for clients who want proof of hours

Hubstaff is not really a freelancer's tracker — it is a workforce tool that freelancers end up using because a client asked for it. It records time, keyboard and mouse activity levels, and optional screenshots, and turns all of it into timesheets a client can inspect. If your client wants proof, not just a number, this is the tool they usually mean.

There is no free plan — only a 14-day trial. Starter is $4.99 per seat a month billed annually ($7 monthly) with time and activity tracking, timesheets, 500 screenshots per seat a month and client invoices for up to 5 clients. Grow is $7.50 annual ($9 monthly) with 1,500 screenshots, one integration, project budgets and expenses. Team is $10 annual ($12 monthly) with unlimited screenshots and integrations, payroll, scheduling and approvals. Enterprise is $25 a seat a month, billed annually only. Starter, Grow and Team all carry a 2-seat minimum, so a solo freelancer's cheapest real bill is $9.98 a month. Add-ons (Insights, Locations, extra screenshots) cost extra per seat.

The limitation to know: screenshots and activity monitoring change the relationship with a client — some welcome the transparency, others read it as surveillance. Also, owners and managers count as paid seats even if they never start a timer. And as of late November 2026 Hubstaff is running a 30% Black Friday discount on annual plans, ending December 1 — worth checking before you pay full price, but not a reason to pick it over a better fit.

Best for: freelancers and small agencies whose clients require verified hours, screenshots or GPS job-site records.

How to pick in 10 minutes

Answer three questions and the list collapses to one tool:

  1. Do you bill by the hour? If yes, you need invoicing from tracked time — Harvest free (1–2 clients) or Clockify Standard ($5.49). If you bill per project, Toggl Track's free plan plus a separate invoicing tool is enough.
  2. How many people track time? Just you: avoid Everhour's 5-seat minimum and Hubstaff's 2-seat minimum. Five or more, inside Asana or Trello: Everhour becomes fairly priced.
  3. Does any client ask for proof? If yes, Hubstaff Starter is the honest answer, and you should charge for the $9.98 a month as a cost of that engagement.

Then run the free plan or trial for two real billing cycles before paying for a year. A tracker you abandon in week three costs more than a tracker you pay $9 a month for and actually use — the lost hours are the expensive part, as Daniel found out. Frequently asked questions

What is the best free time tracking app for freelancers?

Clockify, for most people: free for up to 5 users with unlimited tracking, timers, timesheets and an auto tracker. Harvest's free plan is better if invoicing matters more than the user count, since it includes invoicing for one seat and two projects. Toggl Track's free plan is the simplest of the three but caps at 3 users and has no invoicing.

How much should a freelancer pay for time tracking?

Nothing, to start — three of the five tools here have genuinely usable free plans. When you do pay, expect $4–$9 a month on annual billing for a solo freelancer, or $42.50 a month if you pick a tool with a 5-seat minimum like Everhour. Never pay for a year before a two-week trial on your real work.

Does Toggl Track do invoicing?

No. Toggl Track turns tracked time into reports, not invoices. If you want invoicing from the same tool, use Harvest (included on every plan) or Clockify Standard ($5.49 a month on annual billing), or pair Toggl Track with a dedicated invoicing app.

Is time tracking software worth it for a freelancer?

If you bill by the hour, yes — the math is blunt. Losing even two billable hours a week at $50 an hour is $400 a month. Every tool on this list costs less than a tenth of that, and three of them cost nothing. The return is not productivity; it is hours you already worked, finally billed.

The bottom line

Start with Clockify free — it is the cheapest way to find out whether tracking changes your billing. Move to Harvest if invoicing from the timer matters and you have one or two clients, or to Toggl Track Starter ($9 a month) if you want the simplest timer and invoice elsewhere. Skip Everhour unless your team lives in Asana or Trello and numbers five or more, and treat Hubstaff as a client requirement, not a first choice. The tool matters less than the habit: start the timer, stop the timer, send the invoice.
