Best Business Credit Cards for Freelancers in 2026: 6 Cards Compared

By LedgerLane · November 22, 2026

Daniel is a freelance motion designer in Denver. Every month about $1,100 of business spending runs through his wallet — internet, phone, software subscriptions, gas for client visits, the occasional hotel when a shoot runs out of town. For two years he put all of it on his personal cash-back card, which paid 1% on everything: roughly $130 a year back.

Then he did the arithmetic. The same $1,100 a month, routed through the cards below, would have returned roughly $350 a year — nearly three times as much — plus a $750 welcome bonus in the first few months. Nothing about his spending changed. Only the card did.

That is the whole case for a business credit card: freelancers already spend like a business, and the card is the tool that pays you for it. Below are the six best business credit cards for freelancers in 2026, compared on the numbers that decide the winner — the reward rate on the spending you really do, the cap on that rate, the welcome bonus, and the annual fee.

The short answer: The best business credit card for most freelancers is the Chase Ink Business Cash — 5% back on internet, phone and office supplies, no annual fee. If you want one flat rate on everything, the Wells Fargo Signify Business Cash pays an unlimited 2%. The right pick depends on where your money actually goes.

A quick word on how this list was built. LedgerLane takes no money from card issuers, and there are no affiliate links in this article — a card cannot buy its way onto this list. Every number below was checked on 22–23 November 2026 against each issuer's own published terms or NerdWallet's November 2026 reviews. Offers change constantly, so confirm the live terms on the issuer's page before you apply.

Five of the six cards charge no annual fee at all. That matters, because the honest test of a business card is not whether the rewards sound good — it is whether the rewards survive the fee. On this list, only one card has to clear that bar.

1. Chase Ink Business Cash — best for internet, phone and office supplies

This is the card for the freelancer whose biggest monthly bills are the boring ones: the internet connection, the phone plan, the office supply run. It pays 5% cash back on the first $25,000 spent each account anniversary year at office supply stores and on internet, cable and phone services, 2% on the first $25,000 at gas stations and restaurants, and 1% on everything else. The welcome bonus is $750 cash back after you spend $6,000 in the first three months, and purchases carry a 0% intro APR for 12 months before a variable 16.99%–24.99% APR applies. Annual fee: $0.

The catch: both bonus rates have caps. The 5% rate stops at $25,000 a year in those categories, and the 2% rate has its own $25,000 cap — after that, everything earns 1%. A freelancer spending $3,000 a month on internet, phone and supplies would hit the 5% cap in about eight months. Generous for most solo operators, but a cap all the same.

2. Chase Ink Business Unlimited — best for one card and zero thinking

If tracking categories sounds like a second job, this is the Ink card for you. It pays an unlimited 1.5% cash back on every purchase, with no categories and no caps. Same $750 welcome bonus, same 0% intro APR for 12 months, same $0 annual fee.

The catch: 1.5% is the lowest flat rate on this list. The Wells Fargo Signify pays 2% on the exact same spending, with the same $0 fee and the same 12-month intro APR. The Unlimited only wins if you specifically want Chase's points — for example, to combine them with a Chase travel card later. On straight cash back, the Signify beats it.

3. Wells Fargo Signify Business Cash — best flat rate, no tracking

The Signify is the simplest deal on this list: an unlimited 2% cash back on every purchase, with no categories to track and no caps to hit. The welcome bonus is $500 after $5,000 in the first three months, purchases get a 0% intro APR for 12 months, and the annual fee is $0.

The catch: the welcome bonus is $250 smaller than the two Chase Ink cards' $750, and there is a 3% foreign transaction fee — so it is a poor travel companion outside the United States. It is also the newest card here, with a shorter track record than the Ink lineup.

4. American Express Blue Business Cash — best for under $50,000 a year

The Amex Blue Business Cash pays 2% cash back on the first $50,000 of eligible purchases each calendar year, then 1% after that, and the cash back is credited automatically to your statement. The welcome offer is as high as a $500 statement credit after you spend $5,000 in the first three months, and purchases carry a 0% intro APR for 12 months before a variable 16.99%–28.74% APR applies. Annual fee: $0.

The catch: two of them. First, the 2% rate ends at $50,000 a year — a freelancer billing $60,000 or more through the card earns 1% on the last dollars, which quietly makes it worse than the Signify's unlimited 2% at higher spending. Second, the welcome offer varies by applicant, and you may not be eligible for one at all — Amex says so itself. The 2.7% foreign transaction fee is the highest on this list.

5. U.S. Bank Triple Cash Rewards Visa Business — best for drivers, client dinners and software

This is the widest net on the list. It pays 5% cash back on prepaid hotels and car rentals booked directly in the Travel Center, 3% on eligible purchases at gas and EV charging stations (transactions of $200 or less), office supply stores, cell phone service providers and restaurants, and 1% on everything else. The welcome bonus is $750 after $6,000 in the first 180 days — twice as long as Chase's 90-day window, which helps when freelance income is lumpy. Purchases get a 0% intro APR for the first 12 billing cycles. And it is the only card here that pays you back for subscriptions: a $100 annual software credit lands after 11 consecutive months of qualifying software-service purchases. Annual fee: $0.

The catch: gas and EV charging earn 3% only on transactions of $200 or less — a bigger fill-up earns 1% on the whole transaction, not just the excess. There is also a 3% foreign transaction fee. But for a freelancer who drives to clients, eats with them, and pays for software every month, this card's categories line up with real life better than any other $0-fee card here.

6. Chase Ink Business Preferred — best for travel and ad spend

The only card on this list with an annual fee, and the only one that earns points instead of straight cash back. It pays 3x points on travel, shipping, internet, cable and phone services, and advertising purchases, on the first $150,000 combined each account anniversary year, and 1x on everything else. The welcome offer is 100,000 bonus points after $8,000 in the first three months — the biggest welcome offer on this list by a wide margin. Annual fee: $95.

The catch: the $95 fee only pays for itself if you spend heavily in the 3x categories or travel enough to use the points well. And unlike every other card here, there is no 0% intro APR period — interest starts from day one, so carrying a balance wipes out the rewards fast. This is a card for freelancers who already spend heavily in its bonus categories, not a first card.

How to pick in 60 seconds

  • Most of your spending is internet, phone and office supplies? Take the Ink Business Cash and its 5%.
  • You want one rate on everything and never want to think about categories? Take the Wells Fargo Signify and its unlimited 2%.
  • You spend under $50,000 a year and like Amex? Take the Blue Business Cash.
  • You drive a lot, eat with clients and pay for software? Take the U.S. Bank Triple Cash.
  • You travel for clients or run ads? Take the Ink Business Preferred — if the $95 fee is covered by the 3x categories.
  • Whichever card you pick, pair it with a separate business checking account — see our guide to the best bank account for freelancers — so the rewards and the bookkeeping stay clean.

Before you apply

You do not need an EIN. Sole proprietors can apply for a business credit card with their Social Security number. You do not need an LLC, and your business name can simply be your own name. The application will ask for your estimated annual business revenue — give an honest number, even if it is modest.

Expect a personal guarantee. Nearly all business cards, including these six, ask for one. That means approval is based mostly on your personal credit score, and late payments can land on your personal credit file. Good to excellent personal credit is the realistic bar for these cards. Paying on time, in full, also builds your business credit profile — see our step-by-step guide to building business credit as a freelancer.

Chase has a rule worth knowing. Chase's widely reported 5/24 rule means that if you have opened five or more personal credit cards in the past 24 months, an Ink application is likely to be declined — whichever Ink card you pick. Count your recent new accounts before you apply.

The intro APR is a tool, not free money. 0% for 12 months helps when a big purchase lands before a client pays. But the rate after that — 16.99% to 28.74% depending on the card — makes carrying a balance the fastest way to lose everything the rewards earned.

Frequently asked questions

Can a freelancer get a business credit card without an EIN? Yes. Sole proprietors can apply using their Social Security number — the issuer asks for your business name (your own name is fine) and your estimated annual business revenue. An EIN helps if you have one, but it is not required for any card on this list.

Will a business credit card affect my personal credit score? Yes, in two ways. The application triggers a hard inquiry on your personal credit, and most issuers report late payments to your personal file because of the personal guarantee. On-time payments generally do not appear on your personal report — policies vary by issuer — but they do build your business credit profile; see our guide to building business credit as a freelancer.

Do I need an LLC to get a business credit card? No. Sole proprietors, LLCs and corporations can all apply. An LLC matters for legal protection, not for card eligibility — the issuer cares about your personal credit and your revenue, not your entity type.

Is a $0-annual-fee business card really free? The annual fee is $0, but the card is not free if you carry a balance: after the 0% intro period, APRs run from 16.99% to 28.74% depending on the card. Foreign transaction fees are the other cost to watch — 2.7% on the Amex and 3% on the Wells Fargo and U.S. Bank cards.

Which business credit card is best for software subscriptions? None of the six pays a bonus rate on software directly. The closest is the U.S. Bank Triple Cash, which gives a $100 annual credit after 11 consecutive months of qualifying software purchases. For the subscriptions themselves, a flat-rate card — the Wells Fargo Signify at an unlimited 2% — is the honest pick. Check each card's terms before assuming a subscription counts as a bonus category.

The bottom line

If your spending is mostly internet, phone and office supplies, take the Ink Business Cash and its 5%. If you want one card for everything, take the Wells Fargo Signify and its unlimited 2%. Either way, your personal card is the most expensive option here: it pays you the least for spending you are doing anyway. Pick the card that matches where your money goes, pay it in full every month, and let the business pay you back for being a business.
