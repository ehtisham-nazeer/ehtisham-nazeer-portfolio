Best Bank Account for Freelancers in the US (2026): 6 Accounts Compared

By LedgerLane · November 21, 2026

The moment freelance income lands in your personal checking account, you have a problem: client payments mix with grocery money, tax time becomes a forensic exercise, and a late-paying client can leave you guessing about the rent. A separate business account fixes all three — and in 2026, the best ones cost nothing a month.

Compared below: Found, Lili, Novo, Bluevine, Relay and Chase Business Complete Checking — every fee and rate checked on 21 November 2026.

The short answer: For most freelancers, Found is the best free starting point — $0 a month with invoicing, bookkeeping and tax estimates built in. If your balance sits idle between projects, Lili Core pays interest on savings for free, and Bluevine Standard pays 1.3% APY on checking itself. Freelancers who handle cash should look at Chase instead.

How we choose

LedgerLane takes no payment for rankings and this article has no affiliate links. Accounts are compared on monthly cost, interest, tools that replace paid software, and later fees. Every provider below gets at least one honest limitation.

1. Found — best free account for solo freelancers

Found is built for the self-employed person. The free plan includes business checking with a Mastercard debit card, up to 20 sub-accounts called Pockets, unlimited invoicing, automatic expense categorization, real-time tax estimates, contractor payments and free 1099-NEC e-filing — no monthly fee, no minimum balance, no overdraft fee. It replaces the invoicing app and bookkeeping subscription most freelancers pay $20–$40 a month for elsewhere.

Interest is the weak spot: the free plan pays nothing. New customers (accounts opened on or after 1 November 2025) can earn a 2.00% APY bonus up to $20,000, but only in months with $5,000+ in qualifying card spend. Plus ($35 a month, $315 a year) pays 1.50% APY up to $20,000; Pro ($80 a month, $720 a year) pays 2.50% APY on all balances, plus 1% cash back on card purchases.

The honest limitation: cash deposits cost $2 each and stop at $2,000 a week, and Plus's interest tops out at $300 a year against a $315 plan cost — buy Plus for bookkeeping, never for yield. Banking services: Lead Bank, Member FDIC (deposits insured up to $250,000).

2. Lili — best free account for earning interest on savings

Lili was designed for freelancers when it launched in 2018, and it still shows. The free plan, Lili Core, costs $0 a month: business checking with a Visa debit card, a separate savings account, free standard ACH and MoneyPass ATM access. Paid plans stack tools on top: Pro ($15 a month, $144 a year) adds tax categories and automatic tax savings; Smart ($35 a month, $336 a year) adds invoicing and accounting; Premium ($55 a month, $528 a year) adds a dedicated specialist and a metal card — all starting with a 30-day trial and 40% off the first three months.

Lili's edge is yield on the free plan: the savings account pays a tiered, variable APY — recent independent checks in November 2026 put it at 2.25% APY up to $500,000 and 4.00% on the portion to $1 million, though Lili's own help pages list 2.50%, so confirm the live rate on lili.co. It is the only free plan here that pays meaningful interest on idle money between projects.

The honest limitation: Lili is online-only with no branches and limited cash handling, and sole proprietors cannot send international wires — only LLCs, corporations and partnerships can. Its tax tools organize money; Lili is not a tax preparer. Banking services: Sunrise Banks, N.A., Member FDIC, with a sweep network Lili says covers up to $3 million.

3. Novo — best simple free checking for digital freelancers

Novo is the simplest account on this list, and that is the point. One free business checking account: $0 monthly fee, $0 minimum balance, no transaction limits, free standard ACH both ways, free incoming domestic and foreign wires, and a debit Mastercard. It connects to 40+ business tools (Stripe, QuickBooks, Shopify, Square, Xero) and includes built-in invoicing and a budgeting feature called Reserves that splits money into labelled buckets.

Novo's fee schedule (last revised 26 February 2026) lists 0% APY on checking. A separate Novo Savings option pays 2% APY once your balance reaches $50,000, and ATM fees are refunded up to $7 a month.

The honest limitation: your checking balance earns zero, there are no cash deposits, and the fees that do exist are expensive — an outgoing domestic wire can cost up to $45, express check deposits up to 2% of the check. Novo suits freelancers paid electronically, not those paid in cash or by wire. Banking services: Middlesex Federal Savings, F.A., Member FDIC.

4. Bluevine — best for earning interest on your checking balance

Bluevine is the only account here that pays interest on the checking balance itself, on a free plan. Bluevine Standard costs $0 a month and pays 1.3% APY on balances up to $250,000 — but only in months when you meet an activity rule: $500 in debit card spending or $2,500 in payments received from customers. No monthly fees, no overdraft or NSF charges, no minimum opening deposit.

Paid tiers raise the rate: Plus ($30 a month) pays 1.75% APY up to $250,000; Premier ($95 a month) pays 3.3% APY on all balances, per Bluevine's own plans page. Both fees are waivable — Plus with a $20,000 average balance and $2,000 monthly card spend, Premier with $100,000 and $5,000.

The honest limitation: the free plan's 1.3% APY disappears in any month you miss the activity rule, and the paid tiers only make sense at large balances — Premier's $95 fee needs roughly $57,000 in checking before its extra interest covers it. Banking services: Coastal Community Bank, Member FDIC.

5. Relay — best for organizing money into buckets

Relay is built around one idea: a business should have many small accounts instead of one big one. The free Starter plan costs $0 a month and includes up to 20 checking accounts and 2 savings accounts under one login, plus debit and credit cards, bill pay, invoicing and QuickBooks/Xero integrations — the natural home for the Profit First method, with separate accounts for operating expenses, taxes and owner pay.

Interest is paid on the savings accounts, not on checking: Starter earns 1.19% APY, Grow ($30 a month) earns 1.87%, and Scale ($90 a month, regularly $120) earns 3.21%. Those APYs are dated 17 September 2026 on Relay's pricing page and are variable; no plan has a minimum balance.

The honest limitation: Relay is a budgeting system first and a freelancer toolkit second. It lacks Found's free tax estimates and Lili's automatic tax bucket on the free plan, and its Starter savings APY (1.19%) is the lowest rate here. Choose Relay for the multi-account structure, not for yield or tax tools. Banking services: Thread Bank, Member FDIC, with a sweep program Relay says extends coverage up to $3 million.

6. Chase Business Complete Checking — best if you need a branch

Chase Business Complete Checking is the only traditional bank account on this list, and it earns its place for one reason: branches. With more than 5,000 across the US, it is the account for freelancers who deposit cash or want in-person help. It includes built-in invoicing and card acceptance through Chase Payment Solutions, and no minimum opening deposit.

The cost is a $15 monthly service fee, waivable several ways: a $2,000 minimum daily balance, $2,000+ in Chase QuickAccept or other eligible payment deposits, $2,000 spent on a linked Chase Ink business card, or a linked Chase Private Client checking account.

The honest limitation: the fee is easy to trigger by accident — the $2,000 balance must hold at the end of each business day, and dipping below it even once in a cycle costs $15 unless you hit another waiver route. The checking account pays no interest. Chase is a national bank; deposits are FDIC-insured up to $250,000 per depositor.

What you need to open a freelancer business account

Every account asks for the same basics: a government-issued photo ID, your Social Security number, and your business details. Sole proprietors with no employees can usually apply with an SSN instead of an EIN (Novo's own checklist says so); LLCs and other entities need an EIN and formation documents.

One tip before you apply: set up a tax pocket on day one. US freelancers generally owe quarterly estimated taxes, and the IRS charges penalties for underpayment — so move 25–30% of every payment into a bucket you do not touch (Found's Pockets, Novo's Reserves, Lili's automatic tax savings and Relay's separate accounts all do this). Business checking pays little or nothing, so tax money earns more in a personal high-yield savings account — see our guide to the best high-yield savings accounts in the US (top rates up to 5.00% APY).

Frequently asked questions

Do I legally need a separate business bank account as a freelancer? For a sole proprietor, US law does not require one — but mixing personal and business money can weaken an LLC's liability protection, and the IRS expects clean records. Separate them from day one.

Can I open a business account as a sole proprietor without an EIN? Yes, with most of the online accounts on this list — a sole proprietor with no employees can usually apply with a Social Security number. LLCs, corporations and partnerships need an EIN, and you will need one anyway if you hire anyone.

Which free business account pays the most interest? Lili's savings account pays the most on a free plan (a tiered variable APY — recent checks show 2.25% up to $500,000), followed by Bluevine Standard at 1.3% APY on checking up to $250,000. Found's free plan pays no interest unless you clear its $5,000 card-spend bonus. For much higher personal rates, see our high-yield savings guide.

Are these fintech accounts safe? Are my deposits insured? Yes — with one caveat. Found, Lili, Novo, Bluevine and Relay are financial technology companies, not banks. Your deposits sit with their partner banks (Lead Bank, Sunrise Banks, Middlesex Federal Savings, Coastal Community Bank and Thread Bank), which are FDIC-insured up to $250,000 per depositor. Confirm the partner bank's name on the provider's site before depositing.

The bottom line

There is no single best bank account for freelancers — there is a best one for how you get paid. For banking, invoicing and tax estimates in one free app, open Found. If your balance sits idle, Lili Core or Bluevine Standard will pay you for it. For the simplest free checking that talks to your tools, Novo is the pick. If you run your money in buckets, Relay's 20 accounts are built for that. And if you handle cash or want a branch, Chase Business Complete Checking is worth its $15 fee — as long as you keep the $2,000 balance that waives it.

Whatever you choose, open it before your next client payment lands — the account costs nothing, and clean books are worth far more.
